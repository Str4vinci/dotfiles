# Source-backed style references

Use the target journal's instructions as the controlling authority. These references provide general support for the house style and reporting workflow.

- [NIST Guide to the SI, Chapter 7](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811/nist-guide-si-chapter-7-rules-and-style-conventions-expressing-values): spacing between numerical values and unit symbols, plane-angle exceptions, and conventions for expressing quantities.
- [BIPM SI Brochure](https://www.bipm.org/en/publications/si-brochure): international SI definitions, symbols, and conventions.
- [EASE Guidelines for Authors and Translators](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3467956/): clear and concise scientific English, decimal points, axis labels, units, terminology, and article organisation.
- [ICMJE manuscript-preparation recommendations](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/manuscript-preparation/preparing-for-submission.html): IMRaD structure, reproducible Methods, logical Results, reporting guidelines, and transparent presentation of numerical results.
- [EQUATOR Network reporting-guideline library](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/): selection of reporting checklists by study type.
- [Nature reporting standards](https://www.nature.com/ng/editorial-policies/reporting-standards): examples of expectations for transparency, reproducibility, data availability, and code availability.
- [Nature research figure guide](https://research-figure-guide.nature.com/): practical figure-design guidance. Treat the target journal's figure specifications as higher priority.

These sources do not establish a universal journal style. Use them to explain or verify a convention, then follow the journal and field requirements supplied for the manuscript.
